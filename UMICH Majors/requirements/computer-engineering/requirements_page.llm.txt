Computer engineering focuses on the theory and practice of analysis and design of complex digital systems, including general-purpose computers and special-purpose embedded systems. CE straddles the line between hardware and software, and partially overlaps computer science and electrical engineering programs. Students learn how to design hardware-software systems from transistors to systems software. Microprocessors are a central topic, and our curriculum includes both designing microprocessors and building hardware-software systems that contain microprocessors. While many graduates work at traditional computer companies, many also go to work for companies that embed computers within other products such as cars, consumer electronics, appliances, medical devices, tractors, and airplanes.
Computer Engineering (CE) Declaration Requirements:
To declare a major in CE, you’ll need:
To have finished at least one full semester at U of M
An overall GPA of 2.0 or better
To have credit (either by transfer or a letter grade) for at least one course in each of these categories:
Calculus (such as MATH 115, 116, 120, 121, 156)
Calculus based physics lectures (such as PHYSICS 140 or 160) or chemistry lectures (CHEM 130)
Engineering courses (such as ENGR 100 or [ENGR 101 or ENGR 151 or EECS 180 or EECS 183])

Computer Engineering Program Requirements
Computer Engineering requires at least 128 credits; at least 50 credits must be taken on the U of M Ann Arbor campus and at least 30 credits of technical coursework at the 300-level or higher must be completed on the U of M Ann Arbor Campus.
EECS Grading & Repeat Policies
All technical classes used toward CE requirements must be completed with a grade of a C or better.
A grade of C- or below in any of the College Core, Program Core, or Technical Electives is considered a failing grade and the course must be repeated or substituted with another option. [Grades of C- through D- are acceptable for Intellectual Breadth or General Electives.]
Pass/Fail Information
Computer Engineering students may elect to take some classes as pass/fail.
A grade of “C-” through “A+” in a course elected on a pass/fail basis is considered passing and will be recorded as “P” (pass - for credit toward the degree and no effect on the grade point average). A grade of “D+” or lower in a course elected on a pass/fail basis is considered unsatisfactory and will be recorded as “F” (fail - no credit and no effect on grade point average). General Electives (13 credits)
For CE students, 13 General Elective credits are “required” to graduate. The CoE requires 128 total credits overall and fewer General Elective credits may be needed to achieve this total depending on individual factors for each student. Nearly any course at the University of Michigan can be taken as a General Elective and this is how you get to a total of 128 credits. You might find that you have AP credits or transfer credits that can be used here. Sometimes students consider classes to balance out their technical/engineering courses, such as marching band, dance, art classes, and so on. Computer Engineering Flexible Technical Electives (7 credits)
Computer Engineering students are required to complete 7 credits of Flexible Technical Electives from the options below.
Other courses may be acceptable with prior approval of the Chief Program Advisor. The basic standard is that a class you are proposing as an FTE should involve at least as much technical content as the options on this list. A rough guideline is that it should involve the use of college-level mathematics. If little mathematics beyond arithmetic or basic algebra is involved, it is unlikely to be approved. All tutoring and seminar courses are excluded.

Computer Engineering Sample Paths
The CE program does not require students to choose a specific track. However, each track represents a coherent course of study on a particular topic that a student may later pursue as a career. CE tracks have some overlap so that (a) students do not have to commit to a single track from their junior year, (b) most students will be able to change their track half way through without delaying their graduation, and (c) most students taking “reasonable courses” should be able to complete a track by selecting two appropriate courses in their final semester. Underlined classes meet the MDE requirements.

Robotics and Vision
This track prepares students to design computer systems that move through their physical environments, recognize objects and activities, and draw conclusions about their surroundings. Relative to the “Computer-Based Control Systems” track, the Robotics and Vision track is (1) more focused on specific applications, (2) involves multi-dimensional geometry, linear algebra, as well as motion and mechanical modeling to a greater extent, and (3) focuses more on high-level algorithm and systems. System Software*
In this track, students develop an expertise in designing software that interacts heavily with the hardware and/or environment of the system on which it runs.
