Cities
Build a strong foundation in how cities work to better recognize urban challenges and tackle them with sharper insight and greater creativity. Students study and visit regional centers like Detroit and Chicago, while comparing and contrasting them with global metropolises. Whether it’s transportation, public space and urban design, or land use and other policy issues, Taubman College’s wide variety of electives will help you go deeper on the urban subjects that are important to you.
Design
Apply design methods as a way of researching the real needs of communities and bring immense creativity to how those needs are met. Through visual, communication, interaction, service, and strategic design, you will fill your toolbox with methods and perspectives that help you become an effective agent of change.
Technology
Whether it is an algorithm that optimizes municipal water flow or the app on your phone that calls up a taxi just in time, technology is foundational to urban experiences today. If you want to make cities more sustainable, just, and humane, you must be literate in how data and code can be utilized to modernize urban systems. Through data science, coding, prototyping, and AI, you will leave Taubman College with confidence and ability.
Collaboration
Cities are the result of many different stakeholders having an active role, sometimes in collaboration and sometimes in conflict. Our curriculum introduces you to a variety of voices and perspectives that shape urban environments. Through group work, you will develop skills in collaboration and co-creation so that you leave here a bridge-builder.
Community and Connections
Our Urban Technology degree is centered around project-based, collaborative work. You will experiment and explore the intersection of technology, design, and cities alongside a cohort of inspired peers who will challenge and support you. They will be your sounding board, your support network, and your friends.

STEM Designated
The Bachelor of Science in Urban Technology degree is an approved field of study within the U.S. government’s official STEM fields list. When a student earns a degree in a field on the STEM fields list, they may be eligible for the 24-month Optional Practical Training (OPT) extension. OPT is defined as practical work experience in your field of study after completion of a degree. With a STEM degree, a student’s “regular” OPT of 12 months may be extended for an additional 24 months. For further details regarding STEM extensions contact the International Center.
