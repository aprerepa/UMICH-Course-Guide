MATSCIE 220. Introduction to Materials and Manufacturing

MATSCIE 150 or MATSCIE 220 or MATSCIE 250. (4 credits)

MATSCIE 242. Physics of Materials
Advisory Prerequisite: None. Enforced Prerequisite: PHYSICS 240; Co-requisite: MATH 216.
Minimum grade requirement of “C-” for enforced prerequisite.
(4 credits)
Basic principles of modern physics and quantum mechanics as pertain to solid state physics and the physical behavior of materials on the nanometer scale. Applications to solid state and nano-structured materials will be emphasized including band structure, bonding and magnetic, optical and electronic response.

MATSCIE 250. Principles of Engineering Materials
Advisory Prerequisite: None. Enforced Prerequisite: CHEM 130 or CHEM 210. Minimum grade requirement of “C-” for enforced prerequisite.
Credit Exclusion: Student may only receive credit for one course
,
MATSCIE 150 or MATSCIE 220 or MATSCIE 250.
(4 credits)
Introductory course to engineering materials.  Properties (mechanical, thermal and electrical) of metals, polymers, ceramics and electronic materials.  Correlation of these properties with (1) their internal structures (atomic, molecular, crystalline, micro- and macro-), (2) service conditions (mechanical, thermal, chemical, electrical, magnetic and radiation), and (3) processing.

or MATH 120
or MATH 185 or MATH 295) and (MATH 116
or MATH 121

Equips students with mathematical tools needed to succeed in upper-level curriculum in Materials

Advisory Prerequisite: Open only to 1st- or 2nd-year undergraduate students with permission of instructor. Enforced

MATSCIE 281 (ANTHRARC 281). Making Things: Three Million Years of Materials and Culture
Advisory and Enforced Prerequisite: None. (3 credits)
Connections between the discovery of new materials – such as ceramics, concrete, precious stones and metals, glass, steel, plastics, and semiconductors – and social transformations worldwide. To see these connections, the course will fuse basic concepts in materials science and engineering with perspectives and methods from anthropological archaeology.

MATSCIE 330. Thermodynamics of Materials
Advisory
Prerequisite: None.
Enforced
Prerequisite: (PHYSICS 140 and PHYSICS 141) and MATH 215 and (MATSCIE 220 or MATSCIE 250) and MATSCIE 254. Minimum grade requirement of “C-” for enforced prerequisite. (4 credits)
The laws of thermodynamics and their consequences. Applications to solid and liquid materials. Mass and energy balances. Gas reactions. Phase diagrams. Ellingham, Pourbaix and stability diagrams.
CourseProfile (ATLAS)
MATSCIE 335. Kinetics and Transport in Materials Engineering
Advisory Prerequisite: None. Enforced Prerequisite: MATH 215 and MATH 216 and (MATSCIE 220 or MATSCIE 250) and MATSCIE 330 and MATSCIE 254. Minimum grade requirement of “C-” for enforced prerequisite. (4 credits)
Application of basic principles of molecular transport and mass, energy and momentum balance to the solution of heat, diffusion and fluid flow problems relevant to materials processing. Introduction to radiative heat transfer. Empirical approaches to and dimensional analysis of complex transport problems including convection, turbulence and non-Newtonian flow.
CourseProfile (ATLAS)
MATSCIE 350. Structures of Materials
Advisory
Prerequisite: None.
Enforced
Prerequisite: (MATSCIE 220 or MATSCIE 250) and MATSCIE 254. Minimum grade requirement of “C-” for enforced prerequisite. (4 credits)
Application of fundamental principles of crystal structures, symmetry, and defects to understand structure-property relationships in materials. Topics include atomic bonding, amorphous structures, crystallography, diffraction, atomistic descriptions of point, line, and planar defects in crystalline solids, and microstructures. Emphasis is placed on linking atomic-scale structure and microstructure to the mechanical, chemical, and functional properties of materials relevant to engineering applications.
