What kind of engineer will you be?
University of Michigan Engineering offers
18 undergraduate majors
leading to a Bachelor of Science degree, with hands-on opportunities that align with your specific interests and values. Explore your options, ask questions and make informed decisions. An
engineering academic advisor
can help you clarify your goals and choose a path that fits.
Explore majors
Aerospace Engineering (AEROSP)
The science of flight divides into two branches: Aeronautics focuses on designing and studying aircraft within Earth’s atmosphere, while Astronautics deals with spacecraft design and study.

The application of the life sciences and engineering principles to bridge the gap between medical technology and medicine in practice.

Chemical Engineering (CHE)
The application of the natural sciences, math, and often coding to develop cost-effective and environmentally-friendly ways to produce materials medicines, and energy that can improve lives and the planet.
CHE Course List / Bulletin

Civil Engineering (CEE)
The design and construction of structures and systems that support everyday life, including buildings, bridges, and transportation networks, with a focus on safety, efficiency and long-term resiliency.
CEE Course List / Bulletin
Civil Engineering Career Info (PDF)
Climate and Meteorology (CLIMATE)
The study of the atmosphere, including its physical and chemical processes and characteristics.

Computer Engineering (EECS) (CE)
The design and construction of computing systems from the hardware to the software to harness technology in new, productive, and surprising ways.

Computer Science (CSE)
The theory and practice of how to design and analyze algorithms, how to store and retrieve information, how computers function, and how to develop software systems that solve complex problems.

Electrical Engineering (EECS) (EE)
The study of devices, circuits, signals & systems, electromagnetics, and programming, with additional expertise in electronics, power, control, communications, optics, and/or computers to solve problems in any field you choose.

A combination of strong physics education with an individually chosen engineering discipline.

Environmental Engineering (CEE)
The development of technologies and practices that protect public health and the environment, focusing on clean water, air quality, waste management and sustainable resource use.

Industrial and Operations Engineering (IOE)
The optimization of complex systems and processes by collaborating with people, analyzing data and drawing insights from a combined engineering and business mindset.

Materials Science and Engineering (MATSCIE)
The investigation of how materials perform and why they fail so that we can create materials with unprecedented functions and properties.

Mechanical Engineering (MECHENG)
A fundamental field of engineering that examines, designs, and creates solutions for anything that moves. Mechanical engineers work across all industries, from medical and energy to manufacturing and aerospace.
MECHENG Course List / Bulletin

Naval Architecture & Marine Engineering (NAME)
Naval architects handle design, stability, hydrodynamics, and performance, while marine engineers manage propulsion, power, and environmental systems. Careers span shipyards, design firms, offshore engineering, and marine research.

Nuclear Engineering & Radiological Sciences (NERS)
Nuclear Engineering & Radiological Sciences is the study of nuclear, radiological, and plasma processes to solve problems in energy, medicine, security and space.

Robotics (ROB)
The study of embodied intelligence for machines that sense, reason, act, and work with people to improve quality of life and productivity equitably across society.
